The Layoff Audit · A starting point

Got laid off?
Start here.

You don’t need to rebuild your career today.
Let’s get through the next few steps.

By Irvin · Built from experience and the Ex-GH community

Read the short guideHelping a friend?

I got laid off. Then the spreadsheet got out of hand.

What started as a way to keep track of people became a community helping each other find what comes next. Here’s the short version of what I’d pass along. Pick what helps. You do not need to finish this today.

01 / GET YOUR FOOTING

Stabilize before you scale.

You don’t need a personal brand strategy by Thursday.

  • Put separation paperwork, benefits dates, and questions for HR in one place.
  • Review essential expenses and upcoming deadlines. Ask for help with anything unclear before signing.

U.S. starting points: unemployment benefits · health coverage options. Check the rules and deadlines that apply to you.

02 / KEEP YOUR FOUNDATION

Your career needs a home outside work.

Employment is temporary. Your professional infrastructure shouldn’t be.

  • Use a personal email for your search. Update your résumé and LinkedIn with clear accomplishments.
  • Reconnect with colleagues through personal channels. Keep company confidential information out of your portfolio.
03 / MAKE THE ASK

Help people help you.

I call that first wave of attention the “empathy window.” Give people something specific to do. If it’s been months, you can still reach out.

  • Tell a few trusted people what roles you’re targeting.
  • Ask for one introduction, one review, or one conversation.
“I’m looking for [role]. I saw an opening at [company]. Would you be comfortable introducing me to someone on that team?”
04 / FIND THE BOTTLENECK

Audit your search.

More applications aren’t always the next move.

  • Few screens? Review targeting and résumé positioning. Screens but few interviews? Practice your opening story.
  • Interviews but no offers? Look for recurring feedback. Track patterns, then adjust one thing.

These are clues, not a verdict. Hiring decisions also reflect competition and circumstances you can’t see.

05 / BUILD RELATIONSHIPS

Network like a human.

A good conversation can be useful even when there’s no opening.

  • Ask about their work, what they’re seeing, and where your experience might fit.
  • Follow up with something specific you learned. Let them know when their advice helped.
06 / GIVE YOURSELF A SHOT

Don’t disqualify yourself before they do.

Read the actual work behind the title.

  • Separate essential requirements from things you could learn.
  • Apply where you can show relevant impact. Translate your experience into the employer’s language.
07 / MAKE IT SUSTAINABLE

Build a week you can survive.

You still have a life. Possibly children who would like a snack immediately.

  • Make room for focused applications, conversations, preparation, and time away.
  • On a hard day, one follow-up or one useful application can be enough.
08 / FIND YOUR PEOPLE

You don’t have to do this alone.

You don’t need to run a network to be part of one.

  • Find a community where you can ask questions and share opportunities.
  • When you have capacity, help one person with one concrete thing.

FOR THE FRIEND WHO STILL HAS A JOB

Pick a person. Do one thing.

Offer a specific introduction, review a résumé, or share a relevant opening. Then check in again after the initial attention fades.

“I can review your résumé this week or introduce you to someone in my network. Would either help?”

A layoff is something that happened to you. It is not a complete assessment of you.
